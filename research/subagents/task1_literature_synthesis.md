## Claim–evidence summary

- **Subjective hyperosmia is the most robust finding**, driven by self-report/questionnaire data (Nordin 2004/2007; Cameron 2007; Muluh 2024; Albaugh 2022), peaks early, odor-specific, favouring food and noxious smells (coffee, meat, cigarette smoke, spoilage). It dissociates from all objective measures in the same cohorts.
- **Objective threshold: no consistent hyperosmia.** Meta-analysis null (Albaugh SMD 0.19, CI crosses 0, I²=81%; Muluh null). Only Luvara & Murizi 1961 (1T) and Dastur 2000 support better sensitivity; Laska 1996 mixed (worse 1T, better 3T); several report worse late-pregnancy thresholds (Ochsenbein-Kölble 2007; Hansen & Glass 1936; Noferi & Giudizi 1946). All threshold tests used lab odors (PEA rose-like, n-butanol) that are non-food-salient — flagged by Albaugh as a possible selection artefact.
- **Identification is the one consistent objective decrement** (Albaugh SMD −0.32; Muluh worse), no postpartum deficit. Trimester specificity inconsistent: Albaugh no trimester effect; Şimşek 2015 1T-only. Sparse evidence of selective better ID for a few odors (clove, strawberry, watermelon).
- **Discrimination uniformly null** (Albaugh SMD 0.004; all four SST studies null), base only 4–5 studies, all n-butanol/SST.
- **Hedonics is where subjective and objective data converge** (reduced pleasantness of select food/noxious odors: Kölble 2001, Ochsenbein-Kölble 2007, Cameron 2007, Gilbert & Wysocki 1991, Swallow 2005a), yet the only meta-analysed odor (coffee) is null and extremely heterogeneous (SMD −0.70, I²=97%, 2 studies). Disgust elevated in 1T (Fessler 2005); Kwon 2025 links odor aversion to Th1 inflammation; disgust never measured in any objective olfaction study — largest evidence gap.
