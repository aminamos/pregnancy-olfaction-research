## Strongest for / against each claim

- **C1** — For: Nordin 2004 (67% early ↑sensitivity, 76% any abnormality, longitudinal). Against: Gilbert & Wysocki 1991 (n=13,610; self-rating lower, not higher).
- **C2** — For: Luvara & Murizi 1961 (only clear positive; blast-injection, confounded by engorgement). Against: Albaugh 2022 pooled meta (OT p=.38) and Cameron 2014 (PEA threshold + d′ null); cleanest single null = Cameron 2014.
- **C3** — For: Muluh 2024 (20-study meta) and Albaugh 2022 (SMD −0.32, p=.05). Against: Fornazieri 2019 (n=124, no OID difference across trimesters).
- **C4** — For: Cameron 2007 (90% named less-pleasant odors) / Kölble 2001 (cigarette, coffee, rum ↓). Against: Albaugh 2022 coffee meta (p=.48) and Laska 1996 (only peanut consistent).

## Trimester specificity (where stance flips)

- **Self-report peaks early then fades:** Nordin 2004 — ↑sensitivity "very common at the early stage (67%)… less common at the late pregnancy stage and almost absent post partum." Ochsenbein-Kölble 2007 instead found self-rated sensitivity higher later/postpartum (contradictory).
- **Thresholds flip by stage:** Laska 1996 higher (worse) T1 → lower (better) T3; Ochsenbein-Kölble 2007 change only in T3/postpartum. Early = null-to-worse; late = weak improvement.
- **Intensity and ID dissociate:** intensity ↑ in T2/T3 (Muluh 2024) yet self-report fades late; ID deficit is T1-specific (Şimşek 2015; Kölble 2001) with null in 2T/3T. Hedonic dip: coffee worse T1 (Ochsenbein), general pleasantness dip T2 (Muluh) — no single consistent trimester.

## Resolution — central-appraisal model

- **No peripheral gain:** detection thresholds and early olfactory ERP (N1/P1) unchanged across pregnancy (Cameron 2014; Sabiniewicz 2024; Olofsson 2005), so self-reported "hyperosmia" is not sensory amplification.
- **The self-report signal is criterion/attention, not sensitivity:** Cameron 2014 found a more liberal response criterion (more false alarms) early in pregnancy with no d′ change; Olofsson 2005 localized the difference to the cognitive P3, not sensory components — consistent with hCG-timed disgust/aversion boosting salience.
- **Appraisal is selective and valence-centered:** increased perceived intensity (T2/T3) plus odor-specific hedonic downgrading and worse identification (a semantic/verbal-fluency load) coexist with null thresholds — i.e., pregnancy alters central appraisal of salient/aversive odors, not the olfactory detection floor.

Unknowns: Muluh detection-threshold outcome (full text); Fornazieri self-report (C1); Heinrichs 2002 and Hummel 2002 test no claim-level olfactory outcomes (NVP link only).
