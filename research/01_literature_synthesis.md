## Claim-evidence summary

- **Subjective hyperosmia:** SUPPORTED. Nordin 2004 (67% early / 76% any), Cameron 2007 (85% name ≥1 odor, 90% name a less-pleasant odor), Nordin 2007 (odor-set specific). Counter: Gilbert & Wysocki 1991 (13,610) rated own smell LOWER; Kölble 2001 self-rating null.
- **Objective detection threshold:** NULL. 6-study meta SMD 0.19 [−0.23, 0.61]; PEA studies null (Cameron 2014a/b, Dastur unpublished aside); only Luvara & Murizi 1961 positive, blast-injection confounded by engorgement.
- **Odor identification:** WORSE. Meta SMD −0.32 [−0.54, −0.09], k=8, n=506/333; trimester does not moderate (Q_M=0.25, p=0.88); SST > UPSIT effect (Q_M=15.44, p<0.004).
- **Discrimination:** NULL. SMD 0.004 [−0.19, 0.19], k=4, I²=0.
- **Hedonics:** MIXED/odor-specific. Coffee meta null (SMD −0.70 [−2.64, 1.23], k=2, I²=97.25%) but individual studies show coffee/cigarette/rum/clove less pleasant (Kölble 2001, Ochsenbein-Kölble 2007, Gilbert & Wysocki 1991).
- **Disgust:** SUPPORTED (single study). Fessler 2005 n=496, 1T highest.

## Repo-specific note

All threshold tests used non-food monomolecular test odors (PEA rose; n-butanol) while complaints name food/spoilage (cooking, coffee, meat, spoiled food). Albaugh 2022 states this explicitly: "monomolecular odor stimuli used to assess odor detection thresholds are likely not contextually relevant." This is the odor-relevance gap (see `04`).
