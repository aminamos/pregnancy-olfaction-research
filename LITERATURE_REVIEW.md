# Literature Review: Olfactory Change in Early Pregnancy

## Abstract

Self-reported smell hypersensitivity in the first trimester is among the most consistent findings in chemosensory self-report (majority of pregnant women, peaking weeks 6-12). Objective testing tells the opposite story: the only meta-analysis in the field finds no threshold improvement and significantly worse odor identification in pregnancy. The defensible model is not a better nose but amplified central appraisal — lower pleasantness, higher disgust, liberal detection criterion — with hCG timing, immune signaling, and conditioned aversion as the live mechanistic leads. No study has ever measured hormones and smell function concomitantly in pregnant women.

## Key Papers

- Cameron (2014), Front Psychol 5:67 — flagship review. ~2/3 report hyperosmia early (Nordin 2004: 67%; Cameron 2007: 85% name ≥1 odor — cooking, smoke, spoiled food, perfume, coffee). Threshold studies (Kölble 2001; Savovic 2002; Laska 1996; Cameron 2014 PEA + signal detection) show no d′ change; only a nonsignificant liberal-criterion trend early. Sole pro-hyperosmia threshold result is Luvara & Murizi 1961, confounded by blast-injection + nasal engorgement. Full text: PMC3915141.
- Albaugh et al. (2022), Chem Senses — first meta-analysis. 13 studies, 523 pregnant / 365 controls. Odor identification worse (SMD −0.32); thresholds, discrimination, coffee hedonics null. Attributes OID deficit to pregnancy cognitive impairment + grey-matter remodeling (Hoekzema 2017) overlapping olfactory cortex. Full text: PMC9780746.
- Nordin et al. (2004), Chem Senses — longitudinal self-report, n=144. 76% report smell/taste abnormality; 67% early hyperosmia; 17% distortions, 14% phantoms; resolves postpartum.
- Gilbert & Wysocki (1991) — Nat Geo Smell Study counterpoint: 13,610 pregnant women rated their own smell lower than controls. Largest N in the field, cuts against the narrative.
- Flaxman & Sherman (2000), Q Rev Biol; Sherman & Flaxman (2002), AJOG; Sherman & Flaxman (2008), Am Nat — embryo-protection synthesis: NVP peaks in organogenesis window, predicts lower miscarriage (9/9 studies), cross-cultural meat-staple correlation, rejects hormone-byproduct and genetic-conflict alternatives on timing grounds.
- Profet (1992) — original teratogen-avoidance proposal (via Cameron review).
- Fessler et al. (2005) — Disgust Scale n=496, first trimester scores highest.
- Cameron (2014), Chem Senses 39:143-150 — PEA thresholds + 75-trial signal detection, longitudinal. Null on sensitivity; liberal criterion trend.
- Olofsson et al. (2005) — chemosensory ERPs: N1/P1 (sensory) unchanged, P3 (cognitive) tendency. High-level locus.
- 2024 EEG study (PMC11219288) — olfactory ERPs unchanged in pregnancy; trigeminal sensitivity decreased. Most "smell" complaints may be trigeminal/irritant, not olfactory.
- "Of scents and cytokines" (2025), Evol Med Public Health — n=58, aversions track plasma cytokines weeks 5-17. Newest mechanistic thread: inflammatory mediation.
- Shingo et al. (2003); Science abo5199 (~2022) — prolactin-driven SVZ neurogenesis in mouse pregnancy, OB interneurons functional near birth. Rodent-only; human adult OB neurogenesis still disputed.

## Consensus

Subjective hyperosmia is real and early-peaking; objective hyperosmia does not exist in the data; hedonic downgrading (coffee, cigarettes, meat, fumes rated less pleasant) is the replicable objective change; estrogen timing contradicts a causal role (peaks at parturition, complaints peak week 8).

## Open Questions

hCG is the best temporal match (peaks with symptoms; hCG-diet/infertility-injection anecdotes mirror complaints) with zero direct tests. No concomitant hormone + psychophysics study exists. Test odors (rose-like PEA, n-butanol) are ecologically irrelevant — no threshold study uses the food/spoilage odors women actually complain about. The nausea link is unresolved: Heinrichs 2002 (congenital anosmics nearly NVP-free) vs Hummel 2002 (no threshold-NVP correlation); Garcia-effect conditioning (Bartoshuk & Wolfe 1990, abstract only) untested. Two direct embryo-protection tests failed (Swallow 2005a; Brown 1997, n=549).

## Solo Research Angles

Strongest solo contribution: systematic map of the hCG-olfaction evidence gap plus re-analysis of existing datasets with odor-relevance coding (which test odors were food-salient vs not — predicts the null pattern). The cytokines paper's reference list is the freshest forward-citation source.
